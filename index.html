A small habit for making room to think

June 8, 2026

Some days end with the feeling that your attention has been spent everywhere and nowhere. You answered messages, moved between tasks, remembered three things halfway through doing something else, and still cannot quite say what the day was about.

I have found it useful to leave a little space between the day and the evening. Not a full review, and definitely not a system that needs maintaining. Just a few quiet minutes with a notebook.

Three lines are enough

At the end of a workday, I write down three things:

  • What moved forward? Name one thing, however small.
  • What is still on my mind? Put the unfinished thought somewhere outside your head.
  • What would make tomorrow easier? Choose one practical starting point.

The point is not to grade the day. It is to give loose thoughts a place to land. When something is written down, I do not have to keep rehearsing it in case I forget.

Keep the bar low

A habit becomes hard to keep when it asks for the perfect mood, a beautiful notebook, or a complete account of everything that happened. This one works best when it is almost too small to count. Three short sentences will do. On a busy evening, even one line is enough.

Sometimes the answer to “what moved forward?” is simply that I noticed where I got stuck. That counts. Noticing is often the first useful step toward changing something.

Let tomorrow stay simple

The last question is deliberately about making the next day easier, not making it more ambitious. I might write “open the draft before checking email” or “ask Mira about the handoff.” A clear first step can save a surprising amount of morning energy.

I do not reread these notes every day or try to turn them into a productivity dashboard. They are mostly a way to close the mental tabs I can close, and to mark the ones I want to return to.

A small pause will not make every day feel finished. But it can make the transition out of work a little kinder—and leave tomorrow with a place to begin.

Starting a blog, one post at a time

October 21, 2026

Starting a blog can feel like a big project, but it really begins with one small decision: write something you would like to remember or share.

The first post does not need to explain everything you might write about later. It can simply be a hello, a thought from the week, or a question you are still working through.

Over time, a collection of posts becomes a record of what caught your attention and how your ideas changed. That is a good reason to begin before everything feels perfectly planned.

So this is a beginning. I will write one post at a time, keep learning as I go, and see what takes shape.
